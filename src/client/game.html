Objective

Finish the job your PDA lists and the way out opens. Each place has its own kind of job (see Contracts below), and its own way in and out. Suspicion maxing out ends the job.

The Uplink, and Records Jobs

Nothing leaves on paper. First find the network closet (UPL on the blueprint; ALM on a job that isn't records) and take the uplink down: while it's online, every station is locked and nothing can be done. On a records job, stand at each records-room workstation to photograph its files. Every capture is written to the log server (LOG), and the logs go out in the next batch. Your PDA counts down to it. Wipe them at the log console before it hits zero. If the timer runs out, or you leave with logs still queued, you're burned: the alarm sounds, hunters come, and it costs your grade, but the job goes on: get the files and reach the way out. (To give up instead, pause and abandon the job.) Wipe the logs, then bring the uplink back up, and nobody will know it was down (+150).

Ways In

Pick your way in on the brief. The main way in is on the ground floor, beside the uplink (a freight lift, a service door, a loading bay, an airlock: it depends where you are): the usual way, and the guards' rounds pass it. From job 2, the rooftop: a hatch on the quiet far side of the upper floor, where you can cut the uplink's line at the junction box yourself, though the exit is all the way downstairs and a cut line can't be brought back for a clean exit. From job 3, the service tunnel: a grate deep in the ground floor, nearer the exit, but pitch dark, with a watchdog prowling nearby.

Contracts

What the job asks depends on where it is. The campus is records: photograph the files. The museum is a swap: take a keycard off the marked guard (a diamond shows through walls), then swap the exhibit in the great hall. The refinery is sabotage: plant three charges, then run for the way out before the fuse runs down. The bank is a vault: the keycard, the vault console, then the ledger. The warehouse is a shipment: read the manifest at the dispatch console, then tag the container it names (a wrong one trips an alarm). The lab is an extraction: take the specimen from its cryo pod and carry it out. It slows you, rules out the grapple, the dash and dragging bodies, and spoils if you dawdle. In every one the uplink comes down first, the HUD counts the steps, and the guide arrow points at the next.

Laser Gates

Some beams ignore your cloak: they measure you, not what the cameras see. A gate of high red beams: crouch and walk under it. A low magenta beam: get flat on the floor and crawl beneath it. A pulsing amber curtain: wait for it to blink off (it flickers just before it comes back) and cross in the gap. The older fixed grids that seal a corridor are the ones a cloak still beats. Catching a gate costs suspicion and the guards come to look.

Twists & Side Goals

From the fourth job one thing is different each time: a shift change (the patrols take new routes), a lockdown (the room doors seal for twenty seconds), lights out (more of the building dark), reinforcements, a light kit (no traps or flash grenades), a clock (the alarm goes up when the window closes), or a silent night (no guards, only dogs that hunt by ear; you can't walk or run, only crouch or crawl, and every step carries: the ring on the floor shows how far. Stand perfectly still and a dog passing by only sniffs). The Daily Heist deals two, never an unfair pair. A chip under the top bar names them. From the third job there is also an optional side goal, such as no alarms, never spotted, no body found, under par, no stun shots or no kit: clear the job with it intact for +250 heist points. The chip greys out the moment it is lost.

Records Rooms

On records jobs a sentry stands in the doorway of each records room and of the log room. Throw a flare down a nearby corridor and the sentry walks off to look at it — slip in behind it, or wait by the flare and stun it. The HUD's FILES counter shows how many are still out there.

Lockers

Records rooms also hold a few lockers, and they're a gamble: most hold loot (+15 score, suspicion eases), but a rigged one costs you 10 score and lights a 5-second fuse on the locker. When it runs out the alarm sounds, suspicion spikes, and two guards appear at the locker, so get clear.

Patrol Bots

Bigger jobs mean more of them patrolling at once. Each sees in a cone ahead of it — the red wedge on the floor — and notices anyone who gets right up close, from any side. Walls block both, so corners and their backs are real cover, and they pause to look around between patrols. Stay in view too long and it reports you, spiking suspicion. If one closes the distance, it opens fire — getting hit spikes suspicion hard, so don't let it catch up.

Heavy Enforcers

From job 9, big armoured bots guard the server rooms. They're slow and see a narrower cone, but their shots hit harder. Three armour plates glow over their heads: each stun knocks one off and staggers them for a moment, the only time a takedown works on one. An EMP drops them outright.

Crawler Mines

From job 5, small spider-bots lie dormant in corridors, red eye barely glowing. Come within a few steps (fewer while sneaking; not at all cloaked and still) and one wakes, scuttles after you, and goes off if it reaches you: a big jump in suspicion that always trips the alarm. One stun or an EMP stops it. Outrun it and it gives up.

Drones

From job 7, drones fly over the walls, sweeping a searchlight across the floor. Stand in the pool of light for a moment and one spots you: it turns red, hangs over you, and calls the bots nearby in. Furniture doesn't hide you from above; a crate, a staff locker, or standing cloaked, crouched and still does. Only an EMP brings one down.

The Alarm

Let suspicion reach 70% and the building goes into alarm: red lights, a klaxon, and every bot moves faster. The first two alarms each send a pair of hunters (dark, fast, sharp-eyed) out of the exit, or up the stairs if you're on floor 2, heading for where you were. It clears once suspicion drops back under 45%: loot lockers bring it down.

Lights, Blackouts & Night Vision

From job 4, parts of each floor have lost power: the lights are dead and it's pitch dark. Guards see much less far in there, unless your torch is on, and they can see its beam on the floor. Night-vision goggles show the dark in green without a torch. They run on a battery that recharges while they're off, and putting them on switches your torch off. Blackouts are hatched on the blueprint once you've been through them.

Every corridor light and lit room has a wall switch. Switching a light off is silent, unlike shooting it out, but a guard nearby will notice and come to switch it back on, so darkness doubles as a lure. From job 10, each floor's lights are wired in circuits with a breaker panel each: cut one and its whole section goes dark, until a guard is sent to reset it.

Watchdogs

From job 3, four-legged security robots that hunt by ear, not by sight. Walking is heard a couple of corridors away, sneaking only up close, standing still not at all, and camo doesn't fool them. When one hears you it barks and runs to where the sound came from, so sneak away from that spot. If it reaches you, the job is over. The ring around your feet shows how far your noise carries while one is listening. A flare lures them from further off than a bot. From job 8 they hunt in packs: every half minute or so the pack regroups at a meeting point and sniffs around there for a few seconds, and a dog that hears you calls the pack-mates near it. See them gathered and the meeting point goes on your blueprint. A trap set there takes the whole pack at once.

Security Doors

Some sections are sealed behind a security door, with files inside. Each door has a letter and a colour. Find the terminal with the same colour and hack it (Interact once, then stay close for a few seconds; stepping away pauses it), then find the matching server room and switch its power grid on. The door opens, the section is mapped, and a beacon marks its records room. From job 5 the doors come in a chain: the terminal and server for the next door are often behind the one before it.

Takedowns

The quiet way. Sneak up behind a bot or sentry that hasn't noticed you and a white marker appears over it; the Fire button reads TAKEDOWN. Press it to drop the bot without a sound. Sentries at their posts, bots pausing between patrols and bots staring at a flare are easy to get behind. Walk up without sneaking and they'll hear you. Each takedown is worth bonus heist points.

Your Gun

Its cells glow brighter as it recharges and the arc at the muzzle lights when it's ready; the pips on the back are your EMP charges. It picks its own ammo for whatever is in your sights, and the Fire button shows which. Patrol bots and sentries drop to one stun shot, which never runs out and recharges in a moment. But every shot is loud: bots nearby come to see where it came from, watchdogs hear it, and it costs your stealth grade. A watchdog shrugs off stuns, so the gun switches to an EMP pulse, which knocks out everything around where it lands. You start the early jobs with one EMP charge (⚡); from job 6 you start with none, and loot lockers sometimes hold one. Hold Fire (or press {forceEmp}) to fire an EMP at anything. Sneaking shrinks how far a bot can spot you from.

Bodies

A guard you put down stays where it fell, and a guard who comes across it goes to look, calls the others over, and suspicion jumps 25%. The Detection % over a body is how easily a guard would spot it: under a working light a guard spots it from as far as it would spot you; in a dim stretch only closer; in the pitch dark, or inside a room, only when it all but trips over it. Furniture between hides it too. Drag it (Interact beside it) somewhere darker and drop it (Interact again); how low is low enough is up to you. You move slower with a body in tow, can't fire, and can't take it up or down the stairs.

Remote Traps

You carry two. Press G (on a phone, Trap in your kit) to line one up: a dashed line on the floor shows where it would roll and a ring its blast. It rolls up to two cells and stops short of a wall, or settles into a watchdog pack's meeting point or a sentry's post on the way, and the ring turns green when it will. Turn to aim, then roll it (G again, or Roll), so you never have to walk up to a watchdog to leave one; or set it where you stand (Z, or Set here). Its camera watches the way you faced, and the feed shows in the corner, even from the other floor. A trap arms itself in a second, and a blue laser tripwire runs across it: anything that walks across the beam, or turns on the spot in it, sets it off a moment later, and everything close by goes down, dogs and drones included. Something perfectly still never breaks the beam, and you can also blow it by hand whenever you like (B, or Detonate). The bang is loud, so guards nearby come to look. Two can be out at once; V switches between their cameras.

Your Torch

It lights your way, but its beam gives you away: a guard who sees it on the floor down a corridor walks over to look. Switch it off (T, or the torch button up top) and guards only spot you from much closer, but you'll only see the floor right around you.

Sidestep & Peek

Q/E (or the ◀ ▶ buttons above the joystick) shift you sideways without turning — useful for slipping into a side passage while you're still facing a threat ahead. While sneaking, the same keys lean you out just a little instead, letting you peek around a corner and let go to snap back behind cover — you'll see more, but you're still exposing yourself a bit, so don't linger.

Cover

Go up to a desk, table, crate, rack or locker and you are in cover: your suit shimmers so you can see you're tucked in. Nothing holds you there: walk, turn, dash, and you are in the open again as soon as you step away. It's the furniture that hides you, not the shimmer. Low furniture (desks, tables, bins, crates) only hides you while you're crouched: stand behind a desk and a bot sees you over it. Tall furniture (racks, lockers, vending machines) always does. Aiming is its own key ({aim}, or Right Click, or the Aim button) and works anywhere: you hold still while the crosshair is up, A/D (or the mouse) swing it, W/S tilt it, Fire shoots, and the key again lowers the gun. When a bot walks past with its back to you, take it down.

Dodge

Double-tap {strafeLeft} or {strafeRight} (or ◀ / ▶) to throw yourself to one side. A bot shot fired in that first moment goes wide. It needs a couple of seconds to recover, and it's as loud as walking.

The Building

Staff lockers line some corridors. Step inside (Interact) and nothing can see or reach you, not even a watchdog; move to step out. Vent grilles at the foot of a wall are a crawl-through to the other side: a short cut past whatever's in the way. With nothing else in your sights, the gun can shoot out a ceiling light (bots see much less far in that dark patch) or knock over a bin or boxes to make a clatter that nearby bots walk over to check. Both are still shots: loud, and they cost your grade.

Crates

Duck next to a crate and a bot can't make you out at all, even in the open — a guaranteed way to break a chase.

Laser Beams

Some corridors are barred by a glowing red trip-beam, and it stops you cold. Switch on your camo and the beam can't register you, so you walk straight through.

Camo

An optical cloak on a battery: about eight seconds on the move, twice that standing still, then it recharges. While it's on you pass through lasers, and standing still, nothing can see you, not even up close, unless it walks right into you, which breaks the cloak. Moving while cloaked, you're only as hidden as you'd normally be. On a phone, hold the Sneak button to switch it on or off.

Flares

Throw one down the corridor to light it up. Any bot close enough walks over to investigate until it burns out — the way to lure a sentry off its door, or into your stun gun's range.

Blueprint

Open the map ({map}) to see the floor plan drawn so far: everywhere you've been, plus whole sections a server grid maps for you, with door letters and marked records rooms. The job pauses while it's open.
